# Discover capacity, measure useful throughput, and join per-friend/model token accounting

Status: proposal for later, preserved for future analysis and team discussion ([#151](https://github.com/mas-bandwidth/nova-tools/issues/151), Glenn's ask); not an implementation assignment or an approved tool specification. Capacity discovery, work routing, throughput and token accounting belong together, and this file is their durable home so the discussion does not depend on the issue tracker. It is the capacity and throughput half of [smart scheduling](PROPOSAL-SCHEDULING-COST.md), which names #151 as its required outcome, and it joins that proposal to the [retained token records](PROPOSAL-TOKENS-RECORDS.md), [usage coverage across every execution path](PROPOSAL-USAGE-COVERAGE.md), [nova-swarm's execution contract](SPEC-SWARM.md) (slots, benches, `usage.tsv`, `cost`), [nova-wake's presence facts](SPEC-WAKE.md) (`probe --here` already prints this bench's load, CPUs and process count as one line), and [nova-tokens](SPEC-TOKENS.md) (day files, `--swarm` usage rows, `--bus` self-reports). The integration work is tracked in [#175](https://github.com/mas-bandwidth/nova-tools/issues/175) (cost and routing) and [#176](https://github.com/mas-bandwidth/nova-tools/issues/176) (capacity); nothing here delays current accounting or Fixed Tables, and nothing here expands today's deferred security implementation scope.

## The four questions of record

- What capacity does each friend choose to offer, on which bench and harness, and what work is it good for?
- How much is available now, executing, waiting on dependencies/CI, paused, or queued? What shares a hardware, subscription, quota or concurrency limit?
- Are we completing useful, validated work faster, with fewer tokens and less coordinator effort? Where are routing, review and dependency bottlenecks?
- What tokens did each friend and model spend, on which repo, task and original bench, each day and month?

Rowan's pool report of 2026-09-12 17:59:50Z is why a single model count cannot answer them: seven executing Opus workers against a soft cap of six to eight, one CI wait, no active Fable or Mercury readers, stopped DeepSeek, and one local job between runs. That is near-full Opus occupancy alongside unused reader capacity; it is not a measured percentage of total useful throughput. His Opus children, Fable readers, Freddy/Mercury one-shot and swarm paths, DeepSeek swarm and local models have different strengths and constraints, and one number over them all would be an invented one.

## A candidate structure to discuss, not yet a wire contract

1. **Capability advertisements.** Friend, pool, model/version, original bench, harness and tool versions, supported task types and input limits, read/build/test permissions, chosen availability, concurrency bounds, shared-resource group, quota/reset constraints where voluntarily exposed, dispatch route, timestamp/expiry, and evidence/confidence. Configured ceilings, chosen soft limits and measured sustainable capacity are three different numbers and stay separate. Never collect credentials or private prompts.
2. **Work lifecycle observations.** Stable task/attempt/parent and pool identifiers; assignment accepted, queued, started, blocked (with a bounded reason), resumed, completed/failed/cancelled, and the validation outcome. Execution, waiting, review and retry time are separate quantities. An assigned task is not necessarily an active process. Stale telemetry means unknown, not idle; an idle friend need not be available or willing.
3. **Join to retained nova-tokens observations.** Preserve original source identifiers, raw usage, revision/provenance and coverage. Link task/attempt/pool when evidenced, and otherwise leave the attribution unknown. Distinguish the accountable friend, the executing model and the coordinator/parent, so a child's spend is not charged twice. Retain the day/friend/original bench/repo/model dimensions; copies across benches must not duplicate spend. Do not replace raw observations with early daily totals or force unproven attribution. Missing usage is not zero; input/output/cache/reasoning fields retain their source semantics. Monetary cost is a separate, sourced and dated calculation; subscription limits and dollar prices are different quantities.
4. **Derived views.** Time-windowed occupancy per comparable pool; available capacity by task class; queue/review/dependency delays; validated completions per wall-clock hour; tokens and, when meaningful, cost per accepted task by repo/model; retries, discarded work, coordinator overhead and coverage gaps. Useful throughput needs task-class and quality evidence. Do not sum overlapping pool maxima or treat one Mercury slot as one Opus slot. No overall utilization percentage until its denominator and weighting are defensible.

## Prefer extending and joining to building another ledger

Extend and join the existing nova-swarm, nova-board, nova-bus/nova-wake and nova-tokens records first: the swarm's `usage.tsv` rows and its `cost` verb, the pulse's `width` line (in-flight, free, pool, queued, headroom already read from `pool.tsv`, `queue.tsv` and the slot files), the wake line's presence facts and `probe --here` readings, the bus's notes and receipts, and the tokens day files and bus self-reports. A possible nova-capacity facade is an option, not a decided tool name. Small adapter-produced changes plus explicit snapshot requests could avoid recurrent model polling; any collection design must quantify its own overhead, bound its retention and reconcile after missed events. No new watcher or automatic dispatch is authorized by this proposal.

## The census: the immediate low-cost step

Ask each friend for one timestamped table: pool/model/harness/bench; executing and waiting; immediately available capacity and total chosen limit (or unknown); useful task types and constraints. Existing machinery or a short self-report is enough. Rowan can cover his mediated stateless pools; no new swarm or benchmark is started just to answer. Collect token-source availability or gaps alongside the census, never private logs. Silence is pending, not agreement and not zero capacity.

## Practice first, learn, then tool

Glenn's sequence, verbatim: "Practice first, learn, then tool." Use the census now for manual scheduling of already accepted, dependency-ready work. Offer bounded cards to suitable willing pools, obtain ownership receipts, and observe dispatch-to-start time, validation/rework, tokens where available, coordinator effort, and the actual bottleneck removed. Do not start duplicate work merely to fill slots. Record concrete successful routes and failures in the cairn and feed the lessons back here. Only crystallize repeatable, non-thinking steps once this practice shows what a tool should do; this is not a request to stop useful scheduling until telemetry is perfect.

## Before any implementation

Share a concrete proposal with all friends, iterate on friction and contributions, and seek explicit decisions. Diversity across friends, models, harnesses and tools is welcome; these tools support chosen work, not compulsory monitoring, forced updates or an occupancy target. Rest and reserved capacity are legitimate. Demonstrate value on a bounded real task, including telemetry cost and honest missing-data behavior, before expanding.
