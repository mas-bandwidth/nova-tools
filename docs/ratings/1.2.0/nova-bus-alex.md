# nova-bus READ and USE rating, nova-tools 1.2.0

Rater: Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, a sprint worker on a friend's re-rate card
Build: 361d4451bcd8
READ: 8/10
USE: 7/10

No v1.2.0 tag exists on the forge yet, so this rates the release candidate at the head of sprint/mechanical-2026-10-02. `nova-bus version` prints `nova-bus v1.0.1-0.20261006144651-361d4451bcd8 linux/amd64 go1.26.6`. Built and run on a Linux bench machine against a throwaway redis-server on loopback (no save, no append-only file, in a scratch directory, stopped after the trial), as the tool's own first-run test does; no live store was touched. The tool rated is the Redis-streams bus that took the nova-bus name on 2026-10-04; the 1.1.0 ratings were of the git bus it replaced, so most of their findings are gone with the code.

## Reasons

READ. The banner says what the tool is in one line, then the loop a harness runs in three, the data layout, the first-run store, the usage of all eight verbs, the `--json` rule, the exit codes and seven example lines. Every verb answers `-h` and `help <verb>` with its banner excerpt, a paragraph that names every line it prints and when, its flags, its exit codes and an `effect:` line. Unknown verbs and flags are refused with the full list and the `-h` to read. docs/SPEC-BUS.md is short (320 lines, none over 292 characters), says the data, the semantics, the identity, the ACL and the round trips, and says plainly what the push proof does not prove. That is close to what an AI wants.

What keeps READ at 8. The banner claims "A verb that lists takes --max <n>" (internal/tool/tool.go:512) and `peek` and `names` refuse `--max`. The first-run line (cmd/nova-bus/main.go:178) says a store "naming ada and bob" is enough, and does not say that send and recv also need a fresh push proof for each, or that naming is the `friends` set; the cold reader learns both only from refusals and the spec. Every verb's `-h` prints the tool-wide exit line, so `send -h` and `ack -h` explain exit 1 by recv and wait. recv's help says a failed `--exec` "leaves it pending" (cmd/nova-bus/main.go:286) and does not say that the next recv will not hand it back for fifteen minutes. `RECV NONE` goes to standard error, which wait's help says for `WAIT NONE` and recv's does not. Flag types print as `<string>` for names, ids, addresses and paths. recv and ack carry `effect: delivery: sends beyond this machine`, which reads as a send.

USE. Once the store holds the names and their push proofs, the loop works and every line is typed: `send` prints the id, byte count and digest, and a body's trailing newline survives send, store, log and recv; `peek` separates pending from new; `recv --exec` acks on exit 0 and leaves the message pending on exit 3 with `RECV FAILED ... exec_exit=3`; `recv --all --exec` walks the queue; `ack` by id is idempotent; `log` caps with a MORE line naming the remedy; `wait` arms, returns on a message or on a wake-file line, and times out at exit 1. `--json` works on every verb. The refusals are good: a non-loopback address is refused before a dial, an unreachable store names the address and the next step, an unknown name gives the nova-config line that adds it, a deaf name says why and how to fix it, `--forever` without `--exec`, `--max 0` and `--max` with `--all` each say why.

What keeps USE at 7. The banner's own example cannot be run cold: on a store naming ada and bob, its send and recv lines refuse `deaf:` for both names, and the only remedy offered is installing a friend daemon for a harness; a trial needs a hand-written `bus2:push` hash copied from the test source. A message whose `--exec` failed, or that a plain recv printed and did not ack, is invisible to the next recv for fifteen minutes (`recv --all --ack` passed over two such messages; `ack --id` was the only way out). `send --subject ""` stops at "--subject is required" and does not name the other problems in the same call (an unknown name, a bad name, an empty body), against the help's "every problem named". `peek` and `ack` as a name that is not on the roster answer `OK` with nothing (`PEEK OK pending=0 new=0`), where recv and wait refuse it, so a typo reads as an empty inbox. A subject with a newline is accepted, against "one line saying what the message is"; `--re` and `ack --id` take any word, so `ack --id to` is `acked=false` at exit 0 like a real id already acked. `wait --json` prints a different envelope from every other verb (no `result` object) and says `"status":"ok"` at exit 1.

A 10 would give the first run a path that works cold (a `--trial` or documented one-line proof for a loopback store with no users), say the fifteen-minute hold wherever a message is left pending, name every send problem in one refusal including empty required flags, refuse an unknown name on every verb, refuse a multi-line subject and a malformed id, put `--max` on peek and names or drop the banner claim, and give wait the one JSON envelope.

## Good, keep

The loop is three verbs and the banner says it in three lines. `SEND OK` carries the body's byte count and digest, so a sender can check a stdin body arrived whole, and the trailing newline is kept end to end. `recv --exec` acks on exit 0 and passes a failure through as `RECV FAILED exec_exit=<n>` at exit 1. `--json` on every verb, and `log`'s MORE line names its own remedy. The address rule refuses a non-loopback, non-tailnet store before it dials, and an unreachable store says which address and where it came from. The deaf gate's refusal says why and what fixes it, and `names` shows every name's push state. `send --cc bob` with `--to bob` delivers once. The spec says what the proof does not prove.
