# Keepalive safety projection

MCFriendKeepalive models one endpoint of the symmetric two-direction protocol. One frame carries a new challenge and an acknowledgement of a peer challenge; Ack abstracts that frame's matching local challenge echo. The trusted routing environment supplies a stable seat generation; peer incarnation is learned from an accepted fresh acknowledgement. They are not authentication. No peer wallclock is used. Own nonce issue age is strictly less than ten seconds; proof age reaches down at ten seconds. Consumption prevents a duplicate echo from renewing proof. Startup and local/seat reset invalidate proof; old frames remain in the finite transport history solely to exercise rejection.

The default cadence is at most one emission per local second; missed ticks are skipped. Each of two local incarnations emits at most two challenges in this projection, rather than the implementation's eleven-slot live ledger. History is bounded at four records and time at Ttl + 2 ticks, where Ttl is the ten seconds of the protocol scaled to five ticks (one emission per tick still); a run with three challenges, six records, ten-tick expiry and fourteen ticks did not finish (more than 127 million distinct states, stopped by hand), so the bounds were cut to what finishes in the 110 s budget. No fairness, transport retention, fleet scaling, daemon availability or real process memory claim follows. Reset does not replenish the per-tick emission allowance.

Keepalive leaves durable sleep and native turn count unchanged. The model starts asleep so a broken wake is directly witnessed. It does not compose the existing awake Friend challenge or MCFriendSleep delivery machines; their frozen sources are unchanged. Native activity and SSH recovery are separate policies and not represented here. Seat-generation mapping, peer-instance discovery, frame validation, singleton and atomic health-publication fencing remain concrete implementation obligations.

The positive configuration requests safety invariants. Nine deliberately broken configurations remove exactly the instance, peer-frame order, seat, expiry, consumption, cadence, sleep, turn or transition guard and name the expected invariant in CASES.tsv. The positive run and the nine broken runs are measured with TLC 2.19 on a Linux bench (2026-10-05) and recorded in RUNS.tsv: the positive run passes (326,158 distinct states), and each broken run finds a counterexample to exactly the invariant CASES.tsv names. Running them found three model defects, fixed here: the module did not parse (a `\/` inside a `/\` bullet needs parentheses); the seat witness could not break (see below); and the first bounds did not finish inside the budget.

Refinement clarification: consumed is a monotonically increasing own sequence threshold within an invocation; ACK2 invalidates ACK1 even if ACK1 was never individually consumed. Peer identity is learned from a fresh accepted ACK, not knowledge of an actual remote restart. A different peer incarnation may prove itself with a fresh local challenge; within the observed same peer incarnation, its frame sequence must advance. Silent peer restart does not instantly clear local proof: timeout remains authoritative. The peer broken case removes that frame-order guard and targets NoDuplicateRefresh. Local asleep here is a durable-session ghost, not the received remote status field. The model retains the last emission tick across authority reset, matching the corrected concrete reset scheduling.

Updated transition guard: first accepted peer proof or accepted peer-incarnation change records the highest own sequence already issued. All outstanding challenges at or below that cutoff are invalidated, including those above the individually acknowledged sequence. A later incarnation change therefore needs a challenge issued after the previous transition. Broken transition witness: issue1/2; accept peer2 ACK1; delayed peer1 ACK2 must not prove life. This prevents rollback using pre-transition outstanding challenges, not arbitrary old processes answering genuinely new challenges under trusted routing. No universal incarnation retirement history is claimed. The concrete owner preserves emission scheduling across ResetProof, matching this model's cadence; the previous reset exception is historical.

Seat witness (measured with TLC on 2026-10-05, found by running it): with only the seat check removed the witness never breaks, because the seat change already cuts every challenge issued under the old seat (the transition fence), so the check is subsumed by it and TLC exhausts the space until its budget. The broken seat configuration therefore models a design that does not know the seat moved: no seat check and no fence at the change. The concrete code keeps both.
